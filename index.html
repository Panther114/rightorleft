Answers and question weights
Method

Answers map to +2 (Fuck yes), +1 (Yeah), −1 (Nah) and −2 (Fuck no). Each question carries a weight on every dimension, between −1 and +1, describing the effect of a “yes”. An axis score equals the weighted sum of answers divided by the largest possible sum, scaled to −10…+10. “Not sure” answers are excluded rather than counted as zero, so uncertainty does not pull a result toward the centre.

On each axis, the questions where agreeing moves the score up carry roughly the same total weight as those where agreeing moves it down, so a habit of agreeing with everything cancels out. Authority questions are drawn from all four corners: state power favored by the left (vaccine mandates, content moderation, emissions rules) and by the right (deportation, federal deployments, library removals).

The ellipse radius equals half the weighted standard deviation of the answer-level positions on each axis. A consistency rating of Consistent, Mixed or Cross-pressured follows from the average spread. Classification uses a 3 × 3 grid with boundaries at ±3.3; the culture reading uses ±3.

The “Typical Democrat” and “Typical Republican” markers are illustrative: they are answer sets built from majority positions in public polling and scored with the same method. They are not survey medians.
